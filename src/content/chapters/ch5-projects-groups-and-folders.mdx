## How Groups, Projects, and Folders fit together

The **Projects** panel on the left uses three levels of containers (<a href="#fig-5-1">Figure 5-1</a> and <a href="#table-5-1">Table 5-1</a>):

1. **Groups:** optional top-level headings (such as `Coatings & Optics`) that organize your projects visually in the tree. Groups never hold scientific records directly.
2. **Projects:** the primary scientific containers (such as `Tribology` or `Co/C`). Every procedure, specimen, action, hypothesis, and wiki page belongs to one project.
3. **Folders:** subfolders inside a project (such as `BN-DLC Series` inside `Tribology`) that group related procedures and specimens. Folders can be nested inside other folders to any depth.

When you reopen a notebook, APS ELN automatically re-selects the project or folder you were working in when you closed it.

## Creating, sorting, and moving projects and folders

Follow these steps to set up a new project and organize its folders:

1. **Create a project:** Choose **Project > New Project** (or click **New Project** at the top of the Projects panel). Enter the project **Title** (for example, `Tribology`) and **Description**, then click **Save**.
2. **Edit a project:** Select the project and choose **Project > Edit** (or double-click the project row) to update its title or description.
3. **Create a group:** Choose **Project > New Group** (or right-click empty space in the Projects panel and choose **New Group...**). Enter a non-blank **Group name:**. Drag any project onto the group to file it there, or right-click the group and choose **New Project...** to create a project directly inside it.
4. **Create a folder:** Select a project (or an existing folder) in the Projects panel and choose **New Folder** (`Ctrl+Shift+D` in the team client). Enter the folder title and description and click **Save**.
5. **Reorganize folders:** Use **Move Folder Up** in the folder's context menu to move a nested subfolder one level higher toward the project root.

<Remember>Changing a project's title or moving a project into a group updates the database record only. Never rename or move files inside your notebook's attachment storage folder in Windows Explorer to match a new project title.</Remember>

## Archiving finished projects

When a grant ends or a material series is complete, you want it out of your daily project list without losing any of its data or breaking links from later projects. Archiving solves this cleanly:

1. Select the finished project in the **Projects** panel and choose **Archive** from its context menu (or double-click the project, tick the **Archived** check box, and click **Save**).
2. To view archived projects at any time, click **Show Archived** in the Projects panel toolbar (`Ctrl+Shift+A` in the team client).
3. To reopen an archived project for new experimental work, select it while archived projects are shown and choose **Archive** again (or clear **Archived** in **Project > Edit** and click **Save**).

<Tip>An archived project remains 100% readable and searchable. You can still inspect its curves, copy data from its viewers (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch7-raw-files-drafts-and-instrument-viewers#inside-the-five-core-instrument-viewers`}>Chapter 7: Inside the five core instrument viewers</a>), and cite its items as reference evidence. Meanwhile, the archive lock prevents anyone from accidentally editing or deleting its records.</Tip>

## Sharing projects in a team laboratory

In **APS ELN Community**, your local notebook has one identity and you own every project in it. In a team laboratory running the Commercial server, each project has an owner who controls who can read, edit, or review records inside it.

<InTeamLab title="granting project access and naming hypothesis reviewers">

Right-click a project you own in the **Projects** panel and choose **Share...** to open the project permissions dialog:

- **Read access:** lets a teammate browse the project's items, preview its files, and search its values without changing anything.
- **ReadWrite access:** lets a teammate create and edit procedures, specimens, actions, files, protocols, and values inside the project.
- **Hypothesis approval rights:** administrators and supervisors can tick **Can approve proposals** and **Can accept conclusions** for designated senior researchers who hold `ReadWrite` access on the project.

**Groups** in the Projects panel are personal to your account. Projects that a teammate shares with you appear at the top level of your tree, and arranging your own groups never changes how teammates see the project tree.

</InTeamLab>

## Deleting containers and using the Recycle Bin

APS ELN distinguishes strictly between deleting an empty container (a Group, Project, or Folder) and deleting a scientific item tree (a Procedure, Specimen, or Action):

- **Deleting a Group** (**Delete Group**) never removes any projects; its projects return to the top level of the Projects panel.
- **Deleting a Project or Folder** is permanent and is refused unless the container is completely empty. Even deleted items sitting in the **Recycle Bin** still belong to their original project and folder, so you must restore or permanently remove them before the container itself can be deleted.

When you delete a **Procedure**, **Specimen**, or **Action** in the center **Items** panel, it is not destroyed immediately. Instead, it moves to the **Recycle Bin** (<a href="#fig-5-1">Figure 5-1</a>):

1. Select the item in the **Items** panel and choose **Item > Delete** (or right-click and choose **Delete**).
2. Confirm the prompt **Move item ... to the recycle bin?**. The item and all of its child actions disappear from the active tree.
3. If you deleted the item by mistake, choose **Tools > Recycle Bin...**, select the binned record, and click **Restore**. Close the dialog to see the restored subtree back in your project.
4. To remove a binned record forever, open **Tools > Recycle Bin...**, select it, and click **Delete** (or click **Empty Bin** to purge all unprotected items in the bin).

<Warning title="files and notes do not go to the Recycle Bin">The Recycle Bin holds deleted **Procedures**, **Specimens**, and **Actions**. Deleting an individual **File** or **Note** attached to an active item deletes that file or note permanently (along with the `Reader` values extracted from that file) after confirmation. Never use **Empty Bin** to delete a folder faster without checking what is inside the bin.</Warning>

<GoingDeeper title="why Empty Bin sometimes keeps a deleted item">

APS ELN protects scientific integrity across the whole notebook. A binned item may be cited as evidence or attached as a run on a hypothesis that is currently `InReview` or `Accepted` (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch10-the-hypothesis-tree-and-evidence#submitting-a-conclusion-and-checking-the-content-seal`}>Chapter 10: Submitting a conclusion and checking the content seal</a>). It may also carry an electronic signature (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch14-completeness-audit-and-signatures#signing-an-item-and-verifying-signature-integrity`}>Chapter 14: Signing an item and verifying signature integrity</a>). In either case, **Empty Bin** refuses to destroy that item tree while still removing unrelated binned items, and displays a message listing which records were retained and why.
