Part 3 · Chapter 10

The Hypothesis Tree and Evidence

In This Chapter

  • Proposing a root hypothesis or a follow-up refinement in a project
  • Attaching experimental Runs and context References to an approved hypothesis
  • Citing items, files, and values as evidence with snapshot checksums
  • Submitting a conclusion and passing the two human review gates

Recording procedures, specimens, actions, files, and values (Chapter 6 and Chapter 9) tells you what you made and what numbers you measured. Six months later, an item tree with thirty depositions still does not explain why you changed the bias voltage in run 12 or which idea that sample series tested. The Hypothesis Tree ties your scientific question to the exact runs, files, and values that tested it.

Why connect experiments to a hypothesis tree?

A hypothesis in APS ELN sits inside a project and links four things (Figure 10-1):

  1. The Proposal: what you expect to happen and why (Statement), plus what changed compared to the parent hypothesis (Change) when you are refining an earlier idea.
  2. The Runs and References: the experimental Procedures, Specimens, or Actions you ran to test the hypothesis (Run), or earlier records from any readable project that provide background context (Reference).
  3. The Cited Evidence: the specific items, attached files (.mit, .zrw, .xrcx), or stored values (HITH_{\text{IT}}HIT​, COF\text{COF}COF, KKK, DDD) that support or refute the expectation.
  4. The Conclusion: what you found (What we found) and the outcome (Supported, Refuted, or Inconclusive).

A root hypothesis can have follow-up hypotheses nested beneath it. Across a multi-month research campaign, the tree shows which ideas were supported, which were refuted, and how each result led to the next experiment.

Proposing a hypothesis and passing the first gate

To open the hypothesis view for a project, select the project in the Projects panel and choose Project > Hypotheses (or click the Hypotheses button on the Projects toolbar). Follow these steps to propose and review a hypothesis:

  1. Click New hypothesis… to create a root hypothesis, or select an existing hypothesis and click New follow-up… to propose a child refinement beneath it.
  2. Enter a clear Title and Statement - what you expect, and why (labeled We expected on the Proposal tab). For a follow-up hypothesis, also fill in Change - what differs from the parent hypothesis (labeled What changes).
  3. Optionally select a headline quantity (such as Wear rate or Period). APS ELN will summarize the count, mean, minimum, and maximum of that value across all attached Run items right in the hypothesis tree.
  4. Click OK. The new hypothesis starts in Needs approval (Proposed). While it waits for approval, its author can click Edit… on the Proposal tab to refine the wording, and can attach background Reference items.
  5. In the Your decision panel, the reviewer reads the proposal and clicks Approve (so laboratory runs can begin) or Reject (which requires a comment explaining why).

In ELN

Selecting a parent hypothesis row with follow-ups reveals the same three row buttons used in the Projects and Items panels (Chapter 3: Reading the item tree and its row buttons). Use them to Collapse all below, Expand all below, or Focus.

In a Team Lab: who approves proposals and conclusions in a team lab

In APS ELN Community, you review and approve your own hypotheses locally inside the Desktop GUI. In the Commercial (“For Teams”) edition, an administrator or supervisor configures Administration > Approval Policy… (including an optional Four-eyes rule that prevents anyone from approving their own hypothesis). They can also tick Can approve proposals and Can accept conclusions on a project’s Share… dialog (Chapter 5: Sharing projects in a team laboratory). By default, a student with a Standard role submits proposals and conclusions. Their supervisor sees them immediately in the Dashboard’s Waiting for You list (Chapter 3: The Dashboard and keyboard navigation) and in the mobile web app’s Waiting for me tab (Chapter 8). Projects that hold hypotheses display a HYP badge in the project tree, which turns solid when a decision is waiting on you.

Attaching Runs and citing Evidence

Once a hypothesis passes Gate 1 (Approve), its derived status line tells you where the experimental work stands (Figure 10-2 and Table 10-1). It shows Not started when no runs are attached, In the lab · no evidence yet when runs are attached without evidence, or In the lab · evidence in when evidence is cited.

To connect your experimental items to the hypothesis:

  1. Select the Procedure, Specimen, or Action in the Items panel (for example, Deposition 261015A in Study A).
  2. Either right-click the item in the item tree and choose Hypothesis > Attach to hypothesis…, or open Project > Hypotheses, select the hypothesis, open the Runs tab, and click Attach selected item….
  3. Choose Run (an experiment run in this project to test the hypothesis) or Reference (existing context from any readable project) and confirm. Items attached as runs display a HYP badge in the item tree.

Tip

If an unexpected result in an existing experiment sparks a new idea, right-click that item in the Items panel and choose Hypothesis > New hypothesis from this item…. APS ELN opens the new-hypothesis dialog with that item already attached as a Reference from the very first draft.

After your measurements and value extractions are complete, cite the specific records that test the hypothesis:

  1. In Project > Hypotheses, select the approved hypothesis, switch to the Evidence tab, and click Add evidence….
  2. In the left list of the dialog, select an attached Run or Reference (or any indented child action beneath a run, such as Indentation 261015A or Friction test 261015A). You do not need to attach every child action as a separate Run to cite its files or values.
  3. In the right list, pick what you are citing. You can cite the item as a whole, one of its attached files (such as BN-DLC.zrw or XRR-fit_Co-C(261014B).xrcx), or one of its stored values (K=3.05×10−7 mm3/(N⋅mm)K = 3.05 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{mm}^3/(\text{N}\cdot\text{mm})K=3.05×10−7mm3/(N⋅mm)).
  4. Type a short note explaining why this evidence matters and click OK.

Remember

When you cite a file or a value, APS ELN records the file’s MD5 checksum or the value’s stored number on the citation. If someone later replaces the file or edits the value, the Evidence tab flags that row as changed (or missing if it was deleted). You can see right away if cited evidence changed after the fact.

Submitting a conclusion and checking the content seal

When your runs and cited evidence are in place, you are ready to write the conclusion and send it to Gate 2 (Table 10-1):

  1. Select the hypothesis and open the Conclusion tab.
  2. In What we found, write your summary of the results (you can include Markdown and [[item:N]] links).
  3. Under Outcome, select Supported, Refuted, or Inconclusive.
  4. Click Submit conclusion. APS ELN requires at least one cited piece of evidence before it allows submission. The status changes to Needs acceptance (InReview).
  5. In Your decision, the reviewer inspects the runs, the cited evidence (checking for any changed or missing warnings), and the conclusion text. The reviewer then clicks Accept or Return for more work. Returning a hypothesis requires a comment and sends it back to the lab so the author can add runs or revise the conclusion.

Warning: AI agents cannot click Approve or Accept

An AI assistant connected through the MCP server (Part 4) can propose a hypothesis (propose_hypothesis), attach runs (attach_run), cite evidence (cite_evidence), and submit a conclusion (submit_conclusion). The MCP server has no tool to approve a proposal or accept a conclusion. Those two gates are scientific decisions that a person must make in the Desktop GUI (or the Team Lab web app).
Going Deeper: what the Accepted content seal covers

When a reviewer clicks Accept at Gate 2, APS ELN computes a cryptographic checksum over the hypothesis record. This checksum covers the statement, change description, conclusion text, outcome, attached runs, and cited evidence, and displays Sealed on the record. If any underlying record is tampered with or if a cited file or value drifts after acceptance, the view warns that the accepted record or its evidence changed after sealing. In addition, any item cited by a hypothesis in Needs acceptance or Accepted is protected from deletion, even when emptying the Recycle Bin (Chapter 5).
