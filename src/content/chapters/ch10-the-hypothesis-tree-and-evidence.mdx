Recording procedures, specimens, actions, files, and values (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch6-the-item-tree-and-experiment-templates#designing-a-clean-item-hierarchy`}>Chapter 6</a> and <a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch9-values-units-formulas-and-provenance#the-four-value-sources-and-derived-formulas`}>Chapter 9</a>) tells you *what* you made and *what numbers* you measured. Six months later, however, an item tree with thirty depositions does not explain *why* you changed the bias voltage in run 12 or *which* idea that series of samples tested. The **Hypothesis Tree** connects your scientific reasoning to the exact runs, files, and values that tested it.

## Why connect experiments to a hypothesis tree?

In APS ELN, a **hypothesis** is a first-class record inside a project that links four things (<a href="#fig-10-1">Figure 10-1</a>):

1. **The Proposal:** what you expect to happen and why (`Statement`), plus what changed compared to the parent hypothesis (`Change`) when you are refining an earlier idea.
2. **The Runs and References:** the experimental **Procedures**, **Specimens**, or **Actions** you ran to test the hypothesis (`Run`), or earlier records from any readable project that provide background context (`Reference`).
3. **The Cited Evidence:** the specific items, attached files (`.mit`, `.zrw`, `.xrcx`), or stored values ($H_{\text{IT}}$, $\text{COF}$, $K$, $D$) that support or refute the expectation.
4. **The Conclusion:** what you found (`What we found`) and the outcome (`Supported`, `Refuted`, or `Inconclusive`).

<Figure id="fig-10-1" number="10-1" src={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/figures/fig-10-1-hypothesis-lifecycle.svg`} alt="Flowchart of the four hypothesis stages: Proposed (Needs approval), Gate 1, Approved (Attach Runs and Cite), In Review (Needs acceptance), and Sealed OK, with Evidence Snapshot and Human Gate rules below" caption="The lifecycle of a hypothesis in APS ELN. Every hypothesis passes through two human decisions in the GUI: Gate 1 approves the proposal before laboratory runs are attached, and Gate 2 accepts and seals the conclusion after evidence is cited." />

A root hypothesis can have **follow-up** hypotheses nested beneath it. Across a multi-month research campaign, the tree shows which ideas were supported, which were refuted, and how each result led to the next experiment.

## Proposing a hypothesis and passing the first gate

To open the hypothesis view for a project, select the project in the **Projects** panel and choose **Project > Hypotheses** (or click the **Hypotheses** button on the Projects toolbar). Follow these steps to propose and review a hypothesis:

1. Click **New hypothesis...** to create a root hypothesis, or select an existing hypothesis and click **New follow-up...** to propose a child refinement beneath it.
2. Enter a clear **Title** and **Statement - what you expect, and why** (labeled **We expected** on the **Proposal** tab). For a follow-up hypothesis, also fill in **Change - what differs from the parent hypothesis** (labeled **What changes**).
3. Optionally select a **headline quantity** (such as `Wear rate` or `Period`). APS ELN will summarize the count, mean, minimum, and maximum of that value across all attached `Run` items right in the hypothesis tree.
4. Click **OK**. The new hypothesis starts in `Needs approval` (`Proposed`). While it waits for approval, its author can click **Edit...** on the **Proposal** tab to refine the wording, and can attach background **Reference** items.
5. In the **Your decision** panel, the reviewer reads the proposal and clicks **Approve** (so laboratory runs can begin) or **Reject** (which requires a comment explaining why).

## Attaching Runs and citing Evidence

Once a hypothesis passes Gate 1 (**Approve**), its derived status line tells you where the experimental work stands: `Not started` (no runs attached yet), `In the lab · no evidence yet` (runs attached, no evidence cited), or `In the lab · evidence in` (<a href="#table-10-1">Table 10-1</a>).

To connect your experimental items to the hypothesis:

1. Select the **Procedure**, **Specimen**, or **Action** in the **Items** panel (for example, `Deposition 261015A` in Study A).
2. Either right-click the item in the item tree and choose **Hypothesis > Attach to hypothesis...**, or open **Project > Hypotheses**, select the hypothesis, open the **Runs** tab, and click **Attach selected item...**.
3. Choose **Run** (an experiment run in this project to test the hypothesis) or **Reference** (existing context from any readable project) and confirm. Items attached as runs display a **HYP** badge in the item tree.

<Tip>If an unexpected result in an existing experiment sparks a new idea, right-click that item in the **Items** panel and choose **Hypothesis > New hypothesis from this item...**. APS ELN opens the new-hypothesis dialog with that item already attached as a `Reference` from the very first draft.</Tip>

After your measurements and value extractions are complete, cite the specific records that test the hypothesis:

1. In **Project > Hypotheses**, select the approved hypothesis, switch to the **Evidence** tab, and click **Add evidence...**.
2. In the left list of the dialog, select an attached `Run` or `Reference` (or any indented child action beneath a run, such as `Indentation 261015A` or `Friction test 261015A`). You do not need to attach every child action as a separate `Run` to cite its files or values.
3. In the right list, pick what you are citing: the **item as a whole**, one of its **attached files** (such as `BN-DLC.zrw` or `XRR-fit_Co-C(261014B).xrcx`), or one of its **stored values** (such as $K = 3.05 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{mm}^3/(\text{N}\cdot\text{mm})$).
4. Type a short note explaining why this evidence matters and click **OK**.

<Remember>At the exact moment you cite a file or a value, APS ELN records the file's MD5 checksum or the value's stored number on the citation. If someone later replaces the file or edits the value, the **Evidence** tab flags that row as `changed` (or `missing` if it was deleted) so a reviewer is never misled by silently altered evidence.</Remember>

## Submitting a conclusion and checking the content seal

When your runs and cited evidence are in place, you are ready to write the conclusion and send it to Gate 2 (<a href="#table-10-1">Table 10-1</a>):

1. Select the hypothesis and open the **Conclusion** tab.
2. In **What we found**, write your summary of the results (you can include Markdown and `[[item:N]]` links).
3. Under **Outcome**, select **Supported**, **Refuted**, or **Inconclusive**.
4. Click **Submit conclusion**. APS ELN requires at least one cited piece of evidence before it allows submission. The status changes to `Needs acceptance` (`InReview`).
5. In **Your decision**, the reviewer inspects the runs, the cited evidence (checking for any `changed` or `missing` warnings), and the conclusion text. The reviewer then clicks **Accept** or **Return for more work**. Returning a hypothesis requires a comment and sends it back to the lab so the author can add runs or revise the conclusion.

<Warning title="AI agents cannot click Approve or Accept">An AI assistant connected through the MCP server (Part 4) can propose a hypothesis (`propose_hypothesis`), attach runs (`attach_run`), cite evidence (`cite_evidence`), and submit a conclusion (`submit_conclusion`). However, MCP intentionally exposes no tool to approve a proposal or accept a conclusion. Those two gates are human scientific judgments that must always be made by a person in the Desktop GUI (or the Team Lab web app).</Warning>

<GoingDeeper title="what the Accepted content seal covers">

When a reviewer clicks **Accept** at Gate 2, APS ELN computes a cryptographic checksum over the hypothesis's statement, change description, conclusion text, outcome, attached runs, and cited evidence, displaying **Sealed** on the record. If any underlying record is tampered with or if a cited file or value drifts after acceptance, the view warns that the accepted record or its evidence changed after sealing. In addition, any item cited by a hypothesis in `Needs acceptance` or `Accepted` is protected from deletion, even if someone tries to empty the Recycle Bin (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch5-projects-groups-and-folders#deleting-containers-and-using-the-recycle-bin`}>Chapter 5: Deleting containers and using the Recycle Bin</a>).

</GoingDeeper>
