Part 3 · Chapter 11

The Hypothesis Tree and Evidence

In This Chapter

  • Opening the standalone Hypotheses window and proposing a root or follow-up hypothesis
  • Comparing the Community self-review workflow with Commercial two-gate supervisor review
  • Attaching experimental Runs and References, promoting a Reference with Make run, and citing Evidence
  • Closing or accepting a conclusion and verifying the cryptographic content seal

Recording procedures, specimens, actions, files, and values (Chapter 6 and Chapter 9) tells you what you made and what numbers you measured. By itself, the item tree does not explain why you changed the bias voltage in a new run or which scientific question that sample series tested. The Hypothesis Tree ties your research question to the exact runs, files, and values that tested it.

Why connect experiments to a hypothesis tree?

A hypothesis in APS ELN sits inside a project and links four things (Figure 11-1):

  1. The Proposal: what you expect to happen and why (Statement), plus what changed compared to the parent hypothesis (Change) when you are refining an earlier idea.
  2. The Runs and References: the experimental Procedures, Specimens, or Actions you ran to test the hypothesis (Run), or earlier records from any readable project that provide background context (Reference).
  3. The Cited Evidence: the specific items, attached files (.mit, .zrw, .xrcx), or stored values (HITH_{\text{IT}}HIT​, COF\text{COF}COF, KKK, DDD) that support or refute the expectation.
  4. The Conclusion: what you found (What we found) and the outcome (Supported, Refuted, or Inconclusive).

A root hypothesis can have follow-up hypotheses nested beneath it. Across a multi-month research campaign, the tree shows which ideas were supported, which were refuted, and how each result led to the next experiment (Chapter 10).

Proposing a hypothesis and starting or approving work

Select your active project in the Projects panel and choose Project > Hypotheses (or click the Hypotheses button on the Projects toolbar). The hypotheses open in a separate top-level window with its own Windows taskbar button. The window stays open while you work in the main window and remembers its size and position for your next session.

In APS ELN Community, you are the only reviewer, so a hypothesis moves Draft →\rightarrow→ Open →\rightarrow→ Closed with no separate supervisor approval gate. In the Commercial (“For Teams”) edition, a reviewer with the right role approves proposals and accepts conclusions (Proposed →\rightarrow→ Approved →\rightarrow→ In review →\rightarrow→ Accepted; see Table 11-1).

Follow these steps to propose and start or approve a hypothesis:

  1. Click New hypothesis… to create a root hypothesis, or select an existing hypothesis and click New follow-up… to propose a child refinement beneath it.
  2. Enter a clear Title and Statement - what you expect, and why (labeled We expected on the Proposal tab). For a follow-up hypothesis, also fill in Change - what differs from the parent hypothesis (labeled What changes).
  3. Optionally select a headline quantity (such as Wear rate or Period). APS ELN summarizes the count, mean, minimum, and maximum of that value across all attached Run items right in the hypothesis tree.
  4. Click OK. Read-only proposal and conclusion text boxes are tinted with the row’s status color so you can tell draft, active, and closed records apart at a glance. While the hypothesis is a Draft (Proposed), its author can click Edit… on the Proposal tab to refine the wording and can attach background Reference items.
  5. In APS ELN Community, click Start to make the Draft Open so you can attach experimental runs, or click Discard (with an optional reason) to drop it. In the Commercial edition, a reviewer uses the Your decision panel to click Approve or Reject (which requires a comment).

In ELN

Selecting a parent hypothesis row with follow-ups reveals the same three row buttons used in the Projects and Items panels (Chapter 3: Reading the item tree and its row buttons). Use them to Collapse all below, Expand all below, or Focus.

In a Team Lab: who approves proposals and conclusions in a team lab

In APS ELN Community, you start and close your own hypotheses locally inside the Desktop GUI. In the Commercial (“For Teams”) edition, an administrator or supervisor configures Administration > Approval Policy… (including an optional Four-eyes rule that prevents anyone from approving their own hypothesis). They can also tick Can approve proposals and Can accept conclusions on a project’s Share… dialog (Chapter 5: Sharing projects in a team laboratory). By default, a student with a Standard role submits proposals and conclusions. Their supervisor sees them in the Dashboard’s Waiting for You list (Chapter 3) and in the mobile web app’s Waiting for me tab (Chapter 8). Projects that hold hypotheses display a HYP badge in the project tree, which turns solid when a decision is waiting on you.

Attaching Runs, promoting References, and citing Evidence

Once a hypothesis is Open (Commercial: Approved), its status line shows where the laboratory work stands (Figure 11-2 and Table 11-1).

To connect your experimental items in the main window to the open Hypotheses window:

  1. Select the Procedure, Specimen, or Action in the Items panel of the main window (for example, Deposition 261015A in Study A).
  2. Right-click the item in the item tree and choose Hypothesis > Attach to hypothesis…. Or switch to the Hypotheses window, select the hypothesis, open the Runs tab, and click Attach selected item….
  3. Choose Run (an experimental item in this project made to test the hypothesis) or Reference (existing context from any readable project) and click OK. Items attached as runs display a HYP badge in the item tree.
  4. Suppose you attached an item from the same project as a Reference while the hypothesis was still a Draft. Once the hypothesis is Open, select that row on the Runs tab and click Make run to promote it to a Run.

Tip

If an unexpected result in an existing experiment sparks a new idea, right-click that item in the Items panel and choose Hypothesis > New hypothesis from this item…. APS ELN opens the new-hypothesis dialog with that item already attached as a Reference from the very first draft.

After your measurements and value extractions are complete, cite the specific records that test the hypothesis:

  1. In Project > Hypotheses, select the approved hypothesis, switch to the Evidence tab, and click Add evidence….
  2. In the left list of the dialog, select an attached Run or Reference (or any indented child action beneath a run, such as Indentation 261015A or Friction test 261015A). You do not need to attach every child action as a separate Run to cite its files or values.
  3. In the right list, pick what you are citing. You can cite the item as a whole, one of its attached files (such as BN-DLC.zrw or XRR-fit_Co-C(261014B).xrcx), or one of its stored values (K=3.05×10−7 mm3/(N⋅mm)K = 3.05 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{mm}^3/(\text{N}\cdot\text{mm})K=3.05×10−7mm3/(N⋅mm)).
  4. Type a short note explaining why this evidence matters and click OK.

Remember

When you cite a file or a value, APS ELN records the file’s MD5 checksum or the value’s stored number on the citation. If someone later replaces the file or edits the value, the Evidence tab flags that row as changed (or missing if it was deleted). You can see right away if cited evidence changed after the fact.

Concluding, closing or accepting, and checking the content seal

When your runs and cited evidence are in place, you are ready to write the conclusion and seal the record (Table 11-1):

  1. Select the hypothesis and open the Conclusion tab.
  2. In What we found, write your summary of the results (you can include Markdown and [[item:N]] links).
  3. Under Outcome, select Supported, Refuted, or Inconclusive. At least one cited piece of evidence is required.
  4. In APS ELN Community: click Close with conclusion to move the Open hypothesis to Closed · Supported, Closed · Refuted, or Closed · Inconclusive and seal it in one step. (If an AI agent submitted the conclusion via MCP, the status shows Open · conclusion ready; click Close to seal it. Clicking Edit conclusion reopens a closed hypothesis.)
  5. In the Commercial (“For Teams”) edition: click Submit conclusion to move the hypothesis to In review. In Your decision, the reviewer inspects the runs, evidence, and conclusion text. The reviewer then clicks Accept (final seal) or Return for more work (requires a comment and returns the hypothesis to Approved).

Warning: Planned values from an Experiment Series cannot be cited as evidence

A grey italic Planned value written onto a procedure by Project > Series… (Chapter 12) is excluded from the hypothesis headline key-value summary and cannot be cited as evidence (planned_value). Likewise, Close and Accept refuse to seal a hypothesis while any cited value is still Planned; confirm the value on its item first.

Warning: AI agents cannot start, approve, close, or accept a hypothesis

An AI assistant connected through the MCP server (Part 5) can propose a hypothesis (propose_hypothesis), attach runs (attach_run), cite evidence (cite_evidence), and submit a conclusion (submit_conclusion). The MCP server has no tool to start, discard, approve, reject, close, or accept a hypothesis. Those decisions must always be made by a person in the Desktop GUI (or the Team Lab web app).
Going Deeper: what the Closed / Accepted content seal covers

When you click Close with conclusion in Community (or Accept in the Team edition), APS ELN computes a cryptographic checksum over the hypothesis record. This checksum covers the statement, change description, conclusion text, outcome, attached runs, and cited evidence, and displays Sealed on the record. If any underlying record is tampered with or if a cited file or value drifts after sealing, the view warns that the record or its evidence changed. In addition, any item cited by a hypothesis in InReview or Accepted (Closed) is protected from deletion, even when emptying the Recycle Bin (Chapter 5).
