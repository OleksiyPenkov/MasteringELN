Part 2 · Chapter 5

Projects, Groups, and Folders

In This Chapter

  • Creating and organizing Projects, Groups, and Folders in the left panel
  • Archiving finished projects without losing access to their records
  • Sharing projects and setting review permissions in a team laboratory
  • Restoring deleted items from the Recycle Bin safely

When you open your notebook (Chapter 2: Two ways ELN stores your laboratory work), you first select a project or folder in the left panel (Chapter 3: The three-panel workspace). Keeping that left panel clean lets you find a specimen in two seconds instead of scrolling through hundreds of unrelated depositions.

How Groups, Projects, and Folders fit together

The Projects panel on the left uses three levels of containers (Figure 5-1 and Table 5-1):

  1. Groups: optional top-level headings (such as Coatings & Optics) that group your projects visually in the tree. Groups never hold scientific records directly.
  2. Projects: your main research topics (such as Tribology or Co/C). Every procedure, specimen, action, hypothesis, and wiki page belongs to one project.
  3. Folders: subfolders inside a project (such as BN-DLC Series inside Tribology) that organize related procedures and specimens. You can nest folders to any depth.

Table 5-1: Containers in the Projects panel.

ContainerWhere it sitsWhat it holdsWhat happens when deleted
GroupTop level of the Projects panelProjects (visual grouping only)Projects inside the group move back to the top level; no data is deleted.
ProjectTop level or inside a GroupFolders, Procedures, Specimens, Actions, Hypotheses, and Wiki pagesAllowed only when the project is completely empty (including the Recycle Bin).
FolderInside a Project or parent FolderSubfolders and root Procedures (Proc)Allowed only when the folder has no items, subfolders, or binned items.

When you reopen a notebook, APS ELN automatically re-selects the project or folder you were working in when you closed it.

Creating, sorting, and moving projects and folders

Follow these steps to set up a new project and organize its folders:

  1. Create a project: Choose Project > New Project (or click New Project at the top of the Projects panel). Enter the project Title (for example, Tribology) and Description, then click Save.
  2. Edit a project: Select the project and choose Project > Edit (or double-click the project row) to update its title or description.
  3. Create a group: Choose Project > New Group (or right-click empty space in the Projects panel and choose New Group…). Enter a non-blank Group name:. Drag any project onto the group to file it there, or right-click the group and choose New Project… to create a project directly inside it.
  4. Create a folder: Select a project (or an existing folder) in the Projects panel and choose New Folder (Ctrl+Shift+D in the team client). Enter the folder title and description and click Save.
  5. Reorganize folders: Use Move Folder Up in the folder’s context menu to move a nested subfolder one level higher toward the project root.

Remember

Changing a project’s title or moving a project into a group updates the database record only. Never rename or move files inside your notebook’s attachment storage folder in Windows Explorer to match a new project title.

In ELN

Like rows in the Items panel (Chapter 3: Reading the item tree and its row buttons), any selected Group, Project, or Folder row with children displays three buttons on its right edge. Use them to Collapse all below, Expand all below, or Focus (Focus: collapse everything else).

In a Team Lab: sorting and dragging projects and folders

In the Commercial (“For Teams”) client, right-click any project or folder and open the Sort submenu. You can order siblings By Name, By Date Created, or manually with Move Up and Move Down. The saved order is shared across every workstation in the lab. You can also drag a root procedure (Proc) from the center Items panel and drop it onto a different folder or project in the Projects panel. ELN moves the entire item subtree and relocates its stored attachment files automatically.

Archiving finished projects

When a grant ends or a sample series is finished, archive the project so it stays out of your daily workspace without breaking links from later work:

  1. Select the finished project in the Projects panel and choose Archive from its context menu (or double-click the project, tick the Archived check box, and click Save).
  2. To view archived projects at any time, click Show Archived in the Projects panel toolbar (Ctrl+Shift+A in the team client).
  3. To reopen an archived project for new experimental work, select it while archived projects are shown and choose Archive again (or clear Archived in Project > Edit and click Save).

Tip

An archived project remains 100% readable and searchable. You can still inspect its curves, copy data from its viewers (Chapter 7: Inside the five core instrument viewers), and cite its items as reference evidence. Meanwhile, the archive lock prevents anyone from accidentally editing or deleting its records.

Sharing projects in a team laboratory

In APS ELN Community, your local notebook has one identity and you own every project in it. In a team laboratory running the Commercial server, each project has an owner who controls who can read, edit, or review records inside it.

In a Team Lab: granting project access and naming hypothesis reviewers

Right-click a project you own in the Projects panel and choose Share… to open the project permissions dialog:

  • Read access: lets a teammate browse the project’s items, preview its files, and search its values without changing anything.
  • ReadWrite access: lets a teammate create and edit procedures, specimens, actions, files, protocols, and values inside the project.
  • Hypothesis approval rights: administrators and supervisors can tick Can approve proposals and Can accept conclusions for designated senior researchers who hold ReadWrite access on the project.

Groups in the Projects panel are personal to your account. Projects that a teammate shares with you appear at the top level of your tree, and arranging your own groups never changes how teammates see the project tree.

Deleting containers and using the Recycle Bin

Deleting an empty container (a Group, Project, or Folder) works differently from deleting a scientific item (a Procedure, Specimen, or Action):

  • Deleting a Group (Delete Group) never removes any projects; its projects return to the top level of the Projects panel.
  • Deleting a Project or Folder is permanent and is refused unless the container is completely empty. Even deleted items sitting in the Recycle Bin still belong to their original project and folder, so you must restore or permanently remove them before the container itself can be deleted.

When you delete a Procedure, Specimen, or Action in the center Items panel, it is not destroyed immediately. Instead, it moves to the Recycle Bin (Figure 5-1):

  1. Select the item in the Items panel and choose Item > Delete (or right-click and choose Delete).
  2. Confirm the prompt Move item … to the recycle bin?. The item and all of its child actions disappear from the active tree.
  3. If you deleted the item by mistake, choose Tools > Recycle Bin…, select the binned record, and click Restore. Close the dialog to see the restored subtree back in your project.
  4. To remove a binned record forever, open Tools > Recycle Bin…, select it, and click Delete (or click Empty Bin to purge all unprotected items in the bin).

Warning: files and notes do not go to the Recycle Bin

The Recycle Bin holds deleted Procedures, Specimens, and Actions. Deleting an individual File or Note attached to an active item deletes that file or note permanently (along with the Reader values extracted from that file) after confirmation. Never use Empty Bin to delete a folder faster without checking what is inside the bin.
Going Deeper: why Empty Bin sometimes keeps a deleted item

A binned item may still be cited as evidence or attached as a run on a hypothesis that is InReview or Accepted (Chapter 11: Concluding, closing or accepting, and checking the content seal). It may also carry an electronic signature (Chapter 16: Signing an item and verifying signature integrity). In either case, Empty Bin keeps that item tree, purges the other binned items, and tells you which records were kept and why.
