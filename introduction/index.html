Front Matter

Introduction

About This Book

This book shows you how to record, trace, and analyze laboratory work in APS ELN. You start with a sample you are about to make or measure. By the end, you have a complete experimental tree, the raw instrument files with verified checksums, and the extracted and derived numbers with their exact origins. A teammate, your supervisor, or your future self can verify any result in two clicks.

Every procedure in the book is something you do in the program: a menu command, a dialog field, a viewer button, or a tool call through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) server. You learn by building two real laboratory records from start to finish:

  • Study A (261015A) is a boron nitride and diamond-like carbon (BN/DLC) coating grown by magnetron sputtering. You test it for step height, nanoindentation hardness and modulus, reciprocating friction, and wear rate.
  • Study B (261014B) is a 20-period cobalt/carbon (Co/C\text{Co/C}Co/C) multilayer X-ray mirror measured by X-ray reflectivity (XRR) and fitted in X-Ray Calc 3.

Both studies come from our thin-film and tribology laboratory (arXiv:2610.27859). In daily work, students usually attach the raw file under the right sample, and then break the link between that raw file and the final number. This book teaches the workflow that keeps every link intact.

APS ELN comes in two editions that use the same data model and the same 26 instrument viewers. APS ELN Community is the free single-user edition that stores a local SQLite database on your computer. APS ELN (“For Teams”) is the multi-user laboratory edition connected to a shared server. This book teaches the shared workflow once and marks team-server steps with a dedicated icon. Server installation and IT setup belong in administrator documentation.

The book has five Parts:

  • Part 1, Getting Started, explains why links between files and numbers matter and shows how to open a local or team notebook. It also tours the main window and walks you through recording Study A (261015A) in twenty minutes.
  • Part 2, Recording Daily Lab Work, covers projects, folders, the Procedure -> Specimen -> Action hierarchy, and experiment templates. It also shows you how to use the 26 instrument viewers, working drafts, protocols, and value extraction (Reader, Derived, and Manual).
  • Part 3, Thinking, Searching, and Reviewing, covers the hypothesis tree, the project wiki, tags and links, full-text and semantic search, correlation charts, completeness rules, and electronic signatures.
  • Part 4, Working with an AI Agent (MCP), shows how to connect Claude Code or Codex to your notebook and audit storage and value drift. It also covers the Study B (261014B) XRR fitting workflow and the human review gates that remain in the Desktop GUI.
  • Part 5, The Part of Tens, gives three short checklists for avoiding broken links, preparing a record for supervisor approval, and working safely with an AI agent.

Conventions used in this book

  • Menu commands are in bold, with > between the levels. Item > New Procedure means: open the Item menu and choose New Procedure. Buttons, tab names, dialogs, and field labels are also in bold, spelled as the program shows them.
  • File names, extensions, reader IDs, and MCP tool names appear in code font: agent_tribo.sqlite, .depo.json, .fibps, .mit, .zrw, .xrdml, .xrcx, aps.mit, extract_file_key_values.
  • Quantities and units match the value types stored in the notebook: meters (m\text{m}m), nanometers (nm\text{nm}nm), or ångströms (A˚\AngA˚) for length, and gigapascals (GPa\text{GPa}GPa) for hardness and modulus. Normal force is stored in newtons (N\text{N}N), and grazing (θ\thetaθ) and scattering (2θ2\theta2θ) angles are stored in degrees (∘^\circ∘).

Each chapter works on its own. When a chapter relies on an earlier one, it gives a direct link to the exact section. The web edition and the print PDF edition are built from the same source text.

Foolish Assumptions

This book makes a few assumptions about you:

  • You run laboratory syntheses, depositions, measurements, or simulations, or you are about to start.
  • You know what your instruments measure, even if this is your first week using an electronic laboratory notebook.
  • You have a computer running 64-bit Windows 10 or Windows 11 with the Microsoft Edge WebView2 Runtime installed.
  • You want to spend your time doing science rather than hunting for lost files when you write your thesis or paper.

You do not need to know SQL, database administration, or Delphi programming. Even in Part 4, where an AI assistant talks to the notebook through MCP, you give instructions in plain English.

Beyond the Book

APS ELN includes a built-in manual that covers every menu command and reference dialog.

In ELN

Help > Contents… opens the built-in Help manual. Help > Keyboard Shortcuts… (shortcut F1 in Community) opens the shortcut reference table.

The raw instrument files for both running examples (Study A, 261015A, and Study B, 261014B) ship with this book as downloadable .zip archives. You can use them to complete both practice records in your own account:

  • Study A (261015A, 11 raw files): Download Study-A-261015A-raw.zip (manifest: Study A checksums (/data/study-a/checksums.md5)).
  • Study B (261014B, 7 raw files for agent fitting): Download Study-B-261014B-raw.zip (manifest: Study B checksums (/data/study-b/checksums.md5)). The .xrcx fit file is omitted from the zip so you fit XRR-1_Co-C(261014B).xrdml yourself via XRC_MCP in Chapter 17.
  • Laboratory onboarding assignment and cheat sheet: see the Mandatory ELN Practice Assignment, download the ELN-Mandatory-Assignment.pdf handout, or print the 1-page ELN-Cheat-Sheet.pdf workflow reference.

In the web edition of this book, you can download Study-A-261015A-raw.zip (11 raw files), Study-B-261014B-raw.zip (7 raw files, without the pre-fitted .xrcx), ELN-Cheat-Sheet.pdf, and the ELN-Mandatory-Assignment.pdf handout. They are hosted at http://10.109.92.41/MasteringELN/, at http://10.109.92.41/MasteringELN/data/, at http://10.109.92.41/MasteringELN/assignment/, and in the Zenodo deposit (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23466959).

The source code for APS ELN Community is at github.com/APS-Techno/ELN-Community. If you use APS ELN in published research, please cite the paper listed in the Sources section.

Where to Go from Here

Pick the route that matches what you need today:

  • You are opening ELN for the first time: start with Chapter 1: What an ELN Is, and Why Links Matter. Then read Chapter 2: Opening a Local or Team Lab Notebook and Chapter 3: A Tour of the Main Window.
  • You have a sample to record right now: go straight to Chapter 4: Your First Sample Record in Twenty Minutes. It records every item, file, and value of Study A (261015A) step by step.
  • You want to see the whole workflow on one page: turn to Chapter 1: The whole student workflow on one page (Table 1-1).
  • You are setting up your notebook or checking backups: read Chapter 2: How to back up a local notebook safely.
